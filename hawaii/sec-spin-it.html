    Activity 1.3 Spin It! (20 min)

    Checklist.

    • Practice learning Hawaiian measurements using spinners, creating equations.
      🔗
    • Can play as many rounds as you want.
      🔗
    🔗
    The goal with this game is to have students familiarize more with the measures of length that they just learned, that they can relate them to daily life and culture, and that they can use proportional reasoning to compare them.
    🔗

    Instructions.

    • Spin twice to get two Hawaiian units of length (unit#1, unit#2).
      🔗
    • Roll a 6-sided die. Say, you got a number \(p\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • Create an equation of the form \([p] \cdot \text{unit\#1} = [?] \cdot \text{unit\#2}\text{,}\) and solve it with everyone (approximations and rounding are encouraged).
      🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.3.1.

    • Spin to get these units: iwilei, poho
      🔗
    • Roll a 2
      🔗
    • Create the following equation: [2] poho = [?] iwilei
      (the “opposite equation” is also valid: [2] iwilei = [?] poho.)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Solve this equation using your body, or also drawing tape diagrams.
      🔗
    🔗

    An Optional Challenge: Relate to a Standard Measure.

    Relate to a measure (inch, yard, foot, meter, etc.): Laura and Carlos work together, and spin an “iwilei”. They also choose “inch”.
    • inch
      🔗
    • iwilei
      🔗
    🔗
    They roll a 5. They create the following equation:
    \begin{equation*} [5]~\text{iwilei} = [?]~\text{inches}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Everyone (starting with Laura and Carlos) shares a thing that measures close to 5 iwilei (for example, a car, a boat). Then, students work in pairs to solve the equation (approximations are ok). Each correct answer gives 1 point. After discussing, the answer is made visible, using a diagram.
    🔗
    🔗
