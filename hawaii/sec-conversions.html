    Activity 2.1 Conversions (15 min)

    Checklist.

    • Make a more systematic record of conversions.
      🔗
    • Solve ratio problems.
      🔗
    🔗
    In this activity, students complete a conversion table between Hawaiian measures of length, and make observations about the table. Make sure that students discuss a lot during this part.
    🔗
    Make sure that students know what the table means, and how to read it. Encourage students to use fractions (or fractions and decimals), so that they can see the patterns more easily.
    🔗
    There is a “2” already (which students may observe that it is an approximation), as well as a “1/4”. Explain the meaning of the 2:
    🔗
    1 kīko’o equals to about 2 poho.
    🔗
    🔗
    These are some observations about the table:
    • The diagonal has 1s (ones), because they are about converting from the same unit to the same unit. So the multiplicator is just 1.
      🔗
    • Some values can be deduced from others, without measuring again. (Ask students for examples.)
      🔗
    • The table is “inverse-symmetric” in the sense that a value of \(a\) has a corresponding value of \(1/a\) in the mirror position with respect to the diagonal. This is because if going from one unit of measure to another you need to multiply, then to go back you need to divide by the same number.
      🔗
    🔗

    Teaching tip: make connections.

    Make sure that filling the table does not feel like a routine task, in which the goal is just to complete the table. Relate many of the values filled out, look for more than one way to fill one value, and look for patterns in the table.
    🔗
    You may even surprise your students by introducing the anana (= 2 iwilei = 4 ha’ilima), and include it in the table. You get a “binary system”!
    🔗
    🔗

    Solution.
    This is a sample filled table. Students may try to use decimals instead of just integers in the lower diagonal.
    🔗
    poho. kīko’o. ha’ilima. iwilei.
    1 poho = 1 1/2 1/4 1/8
    1 kīko’o = 2 1 1/2 1/4
    1 ha’ilima = 4 \((2\times2)\) 2 1 1/2
    1 iwilei = 8 \((2\times2\times2)\) 4 \((2\times2)\) 2 1
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Which strategies did you use to fill the table? What was the most challenging part?
    🔗
    Solution.
    • We first filled the diagonal with 1’s because 1 poho = 1 poho, etc.
      🔗
    • Then we filled the lower part of the table.
      🔗
    • Finally we filled the other part with “one over” the upper parts (reciprocals).
      🔗
    • The most challenging: agreeing on how to round.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    What do you notice about the table you filled? Write all things that you notice!
    🔗
    Solution.
    • The diagonal is made out of 1 1 1 1.
      🔗
    • With respect to the diagonal, we always see a number reflected to its multiplicative inverse. E.g.: 4 and 1/4.
      🔗
    • All values are 2 to something! (Even 1/2 is \(2^{-1}\text{.}\))
      🔗
    • For any N, if I multiply the values of Row 1, and multiply this by the product of Column N, I get 1!
      🔗
    • Each new row is “2 times” the previous row. (Same for cols.)
      🔗
